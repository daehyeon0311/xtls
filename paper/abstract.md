## Title

**Exchange coupling reverses the strain route to the quantum-paramagnetic
transition in Ba₂FeSi₂O₇**

## Abstract

> Ba₂FeSi₂O₇ is a quasi-two-dimensional S = 2 antiferromagnet lying close to the
> boundary between magnetic order and a quantum-paramagnetic ground state, the
> two being separated by a critical ratio of the single-ion anisotropy D to the
> exchange J. A recent X-ray linear dichroism study determined D from
> full-multiplet cluster calculations and, holding J at its measured value,
> predicted that compressing the FeO₄ tetrahedron further would drive the system
> across that boundary. Here we compute both quantities from first principles
> for the same deformation, D from a charge-transfer cluster model and J from
> broken-symmetry density-functional theory. At the experimental structure the
> two reproduce the measured parameters, giving D/J = 15.9 against 16.0 from
> inelastic neutron scattering, and place the material at 84% of the critical
> ratio. Under uniaxial strain along c, however, J varies by 68% across four
> degrees of distortion while D varies by 2%, so D/J decreases with compression
> and the transition is reached by tensile rather than compressive strain, at
> most +4.7% along c. We trace the discrepancy to a geometric distinction that
> has not been drawn explicitly: a given distortion angle can be reached either
> by A-site substitution or by uniaxial strain, and the two move the Fe–O
> distance in opposite directions, giving D(Δθ) curves that differ in sign. The
> substitution series runs the wrong way, so strain is the only available route.
> We further show that Kramers degeneracy restricts the transition to
> integer-spin members of the melilite family, leaving Fe²⁺ as effectively the
> only candidate.

## Notes

- The abstract states the disagreement with the earlier prediction without
  characterising it. The mechanism is given, the numbers are given, and the
  reader can judge.
- "at most +4.7%" is deliberate: the bound tightens once the change in α_c is
  included, and it would be misleading to quote it as a target rather than a
  ceiling.
